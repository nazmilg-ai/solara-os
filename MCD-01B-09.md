* child-safety requirements;

This document covers the **standard free-hanging Day & Night Blind only**.

It does not cover:

* Perfect Fit Day & Night blinds;

* INTU Day & Night blinds;
* frame-mounted Day & Night systems;
* dual-fabric or two-independent-roller systems;

# 3. Product Definition

A Day & Night Blind is a roller-based blind manufactured from a continuous fabric containing alternating horizontal transparent or sheer bands and opaque or denser bands.

The fabric passes through the roller system and around a lower section so that two layers of fabric are visible.

By moving one layer relative to the other, the user can:

* align the opaque bands for increased privacy;
* offset the bands to allow light through the sheer sections;
* raise the complete blind to expose the window.

The blind therefore has two distinct functions:

1. raising and lowering the complete blind;
2. adjusting the relative alignment of the front and rear fabric layers.

The fabric follows a fixed, system-specific route through the headrail, around the lower bar, and back into the system. This routing is determined by the manufacturing system and is not an advisor-selectable option.

# 4. Core Product Principles

## 4.1 Roller-based mechanism

Day & Night blinds belong to the roller-based product family.

They use the same core categories of:

* roller tube;
* clutch or motor;
* brackets;
* drive-end mechanism;
* pin-end mechanism;
* fascia or cassette;
* operating chain;
* tubular motor.

The system differs from a standard Roller Blind primarily because of:

* the fabric construction;
* the fixed fabric return path;
* the double-layer visual effect;
* the requirement for stripe or band alignment;
* the Day & Night bottom-bar arrangement;
* product-specific size and fabric limits.

## 4.2 Shared motor principle

Roller Blinds and Day & Night Blinds use the same supplier motor families where they use the same roller tube and mechanism. The motor catalogue should be shared, while finished-blind limits remain product-, fabric-, system- and supplier-specific.

The motor itself does not require a separate Day & Night product record.

The shared motor record may contain:

* supplier;
* motor brand;
* motor model;
* compatible tube diameter;
* torque;
* speed;
* minimum motor insertion width;
* power source;
* control type;
* remote compatibility;
* app compatibility;
* smart-home compatibility;
* warranty.

The finished blind must still be validated separately against Day & Night fabric, cassette and dimensional restrictions.

## 4.3 Supplier-specific validation

There must not be one universal Day & Night size limit.

The permitted configuration depends on:

* supplier;
* system;
* cassette or fascia;
* tube diameter;
* manual or motorised control;
* selected fabric;
* fabric width;
* fabric weight;
* finished width;
* finished drop;
* maximum area;
* supplier ordering restrictions.

# 10. Fabric Selection

## 10.1 Fabric structure

Day & Night fabric contains alternating:

* sheer or transparent horizontal bands;
* opaque, denser or decorative horizontal bands.

The appearance and privacy level depend on how the two fabric layers align.

## 10.2 Fabric categories

Supplier collections may include:

* plain opaque bands with sheer voile;
* textured opaque bands with sheer voile;
* blackout-material bands with sheer voile;
* patterned bands;
* wood-look bands;
* metallic or special-effect bands.

These descriptions refer to the fabric construction and not necessarily the performance of the complete blind.

## 10.3 Blackout terminology

A Day & Night Blind must not be described as a total-blackout blind.

Even where a supplier describes the opaque band as "blackout", light may still enter through:

* sheer sections;
* incomplete stripe alignment;
* side gaps;
* bottom gaps;
* bracket gaps;
* cassette gaps;
* fabric-to-hardware gaps.

The customer-facing explanation should state:

> Aligning the opaque bands increases privacy and reduces light, but a Day & Night Blind is not a total-blackout system.

## 10.4 Privacy limitation

When the sheer bands are aligned, visibility through the blind may increase.

The advisor must explain that:

* daytime privacy depends on internal and external light levels;
* evening privacy may reduce when lights are on inside;
* the blind should be moved to the more closed alignment for increased privacy;
* privacy performance varies by fabric colour and weave.

## 10.5 Fabric viewing rule

Fabric samples should be held against the actual window during the consultation where practical.

The customer should not rely only on the appearance of the sample in the sample book because:

* daylight changes fabric colour;
* sheer sections appear differently against glass;
* external brightness affects transparency;
* darker rooms can change perceived colour;
* privacy performance varies with lighting conditions.

## 10.6 Fabric joins

No fabric-join rule has yet been verified.

The system must not assume joins are permitted.

If the selected finished size exceeds the usable fabric width, the configuration must be:

* blocked;
* referred to supplier;
* or changed to multiple blinds.

## 11.2 System colour

The available colours must be supplier-configured.

Common verified options include:

* White;
* Black;
* Anthracite.

Additional supplier colours must only be shown where verified.

## 11.3 Obstruction clearance

Day & Night Blinds do not have a reverse-roll option. The fabric follows a fixed, system-specific route that is not an advisor-selectable configuration (see §3).

Where available clearance around handles, vents, projecting frames, tiles, or other obstructions is insufficient, the advisor must instead consider, in order of preference:

* changing the fitting position;
* using suitable extension brackets where available;
* fitting outside the recess;
* selecting another suitable product;
* referring the configuration to the supplier.

The system must warn or block the configuration where clearance is insufficient. It must not offer a change of fabric-roll direction as a remedy.

## 11.4 Bottom bar

The bottom-bar type and colour depend on supplier and system.

Possible configurations include:

* enclosed bottom bar;
* colour-matched bottom bar;
* exposed bottom bar;
* system-specific Softshade bottom bar.

The bottom bar is an operational component and must not be treated as a purely decorative accessory.

# 15. Multiple Blinds and Stripe Alignment

## 15.1 Adjacent blinds

Where multiple Day & Night blinds are fitted side by side, the customer may expect the horizontal bands to align.

The advisor must explain that exact alignment can be affected by:

* separate roller tubes;
* fabric cutting;
* fabric batch;
* manufacturing tolerance;
* different blind drops;
* different tube roll-up;
* installation level;
* operational movement.

## 15.2 Same-room instruction

The system should allow blinds to be marked:

* same room;
* same opening;
* alignment requested.

This is a manufacturing instruction and not a guarantee unless the supplier expressly guarantees stripe alignment.

## 15.3 Customer-facing limitation

Suggested wording:

> We will request the closest possible band alignment where blinds are fitted together, but minor variation may occur because each blind is manufactured and operated separately.

# 18. Suitability and Recommendation Rules

## 18.1 Suitable applications

Day & Night blinds may be suitable for:

* living rooms;
* dining rooms;
* home offices;
* street-facing windows;
* bedrooms where total blackout is not required;
* larger windows within supplier limits;
* rooms requiring adjustable light and privacy.

## 18.2 Caution applications

Advisor warning should be shown for:

* bedrooms requiring complete darkness;
* very shallow recesses;
* windows with projecting handles;
* inward-opening windows;
* high-moisture locations without verified suitable fabric;
* doors where the blind may move or strike the frame;
* wide openings exceeding supplier limits;
* customers expecting complete evening privacy while sheer bands are aligned;
* customers expecting perfect stripe alignment across several blinds.

Where a warning concerns insufficient clearance (projecting handles, shallow recesses), the advisor must apply the obstruction-clearance options in §11.3 rather than any fabric-roll-direction change.

## 18.3 Not a total-blackout recommendation

Where the customer requires very high light exclusion, the advisor should discuss:

* total-blackout cassette systems;
* blackout shutters;
* curtains with suitable lining;
* layered window treatments.

A Day & Night Blind must not be sold as equivalent to a total-blackout blind.

# 19. Advisor Guidance

* the blind contains two visible fabric layers;
* moving the chain or motor adjusts both blind height and fabric alignment;
* aligned opaque bands improve privacy;

* privacy changes between daytime and night-time;

* fabric appearance should be checked at the window;
* motorised versions use the same supplier motor families as Roller Blinds;

* the fabric routing is fixed by the system and is not an advisor-selectable option.

# 25. Blocking Rules

The system must block or refer the configuration where:

* width is below supplier minimum;
* width exceeds supplier maximum;
* drop exceeds fabric maximum;
* area exceeds supplier maximum;
* selected fabric is incompatible with the system;
* selected motor does not fit the tube;
* finished width is below the motor minimum;
* selected cassette is unavailable with the motor;
* control length fails child-safety requirements;
* required installation height is missing;
* fabric width would require an unverified join;
* obstruction clearance is insufficient and no alternative in §11.3 resolves it;
* supplier evidence is missing for the requested configuration.
